## Honest lessons from today

1. **A spec audit is not the same as a quality audit.** Today's audit measured what changed between v0.7.1 and v0.7.2 against pre-defined axes. It did not ask "is the deployed site good enough to text a prospect?" That question only gets answered after a full rebuild and a real-eyes look.

2. **Iteration cost compounds.** Six OG mockup iterations to lock the b6 recipe was the right call. But once seven rules were committed, the cost of "let's try one more thing" became the cost of "let's revert seven commits." Knowing when to stop iterating is as important as knowing how to iterate.

3. **A precise spec does not guarantee a good outcome.** v0.7.3's spec was the most precise document the skill has ever had. The implementation matched the spec. The deployed site still missed the bar. Specs prevent ambiguity in execution; they don't prevent the spec itself from being wrong about what good looks like.

4. **"Manual edit per build" is not a failure mode.** It's a legitimate strategy for a 28-prospect month. Skill perfection is the long game; manual touch-ups are the short game. Pursuing the long game while the short game is in motion is what burned today.

5. **Reverting is always available when the skill is under git.** Today proved this. Six hours of work undone in three commands. The cost of trying something risky is now just the time spent — never the loss of the working baseline.
