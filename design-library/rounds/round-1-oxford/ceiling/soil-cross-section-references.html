Round 1 — Soil Cross-Section · Ceiling references
◆ Concept only · Not production art · No illustrator commissioned

Three ceiling references
for the annotated cross-section.

The annotated-soil-cross-section pattern holds a promise: that Oxford can show you the 32 inches under your lawn. The code-drawn version on the Process page is a placeholder. These three concept sketches set the ceiling — the level of craft, annotation density, and material honesty we'd aim at if we commissioned an illustrator. Each is made from SVG + CSS, not painted; they describe a style, not a deliverable. Read them as mood boards with specs attached.

Reference A · Field-manual diagram Ceiling ref · not production

Field-manual diagram

Hatched cross-section with leader lines, numbered figure callouts, and a title block. Reads like an agricultural extension bulletin from 1975 — the authoritative reference most rural landowners already trust.

This is the safest ceiling. Closest to "a real thing you can print and hand to a client." Matches Oxford's editorial voice cleanly. Production risk is low; craft risk is that it becomes too dry.

Medium
Pen-and-ink illustration on cream stock, scanned at high resolution. OR high-end digital that mimics it.
Palette
Cream #f4ecd8 base, four ink-brown strata, single-weight linework.
Annotation
Numbered figures (01–04), leader lines, depth ruler, title block, survey metadata.
Reuse
Highest. Same system can annotate anything: aeration diagram, drainage map, irrigation schematic.
Risk
Familiar. Doesn't surprise anyone. Could read as stock.
Cost estimate
Illustrator: mid. 2–3 weeks, one primary + two secondary diagrams.
Reference B · Painterly strata plate Ceiling ref · not production

Painterly strata plate

Moody painted cross-section — irregular strata edges, soft grain, root systems rendered as brushwork. Roman-numeral labels set in the serif. Reads like a plate torn from a 19th-century natural history book.

This is the highest craft ceiling. Best match for Oxford's editorial voice ("Closing Table," literary). Highest production risk — needs a real illustrator at real rates. If we commit to Reference B, it becomes the brand's hero visual.

Medium
Gouache or digital painting. Scanned grain. Commissioned illustrator.
Palette
Muted lawn green, four tonal earth strata, cream-on-dark labels.
Annotation
Roman numerals I–V, italic serif, mono subscripts. No boxes, no leader dots.
Reuse
Medium. Each new diagram is a new commission. Costly to extend.
Risk
Expensive. Slow. Hard to update. If illustrator leaves, style dies.
Cost estimate
Illustrator: high. 6–8 weeks, one primary plate. Secondary plates are separate commissions.
Reference D · Blueprint / engineering section Ceiling ref · not production

Blueprint / engineering section

Architectural section drawing — dimension lines, numbered balloons, title block, scale, revision number. Reads as capital-E engineering. "This is measured. This is the spec."

Strongest proof-of-rigor ceiling. Pairs well with Class B (Operations-forward) typographic class — even if Oxford doesn't use it, the adjacent SKU might. Risk is coldness: no warmth, no story.

Medium
Vector drawing, blueprint blue paper. Can be production-generated (this sketch already proves that).
Palette
Blueprint blue (#0e2a47) + cream linework. No earth tones — the palette itself is the frame.
Annotation
Dimension lines with arrows, balloon numbers, legend, title block. No prose.
Reuse
Highest. Any diagram can use this system. Code-drawable without an illustrator.
Risk
Clinical. Drops the editorial voice entirely. Not Oxford — more a sibling brand's visual.
Cost estimate
In-house / code-drawable. Minimal. This is the "no-commission" fallback — strong enough to ship as-is.
Recommendation — for the founder review v1 · no commission yet

If we commission, it's Reference B. If we don't, it's Reference D.

A is strong but risks reading as stock extension-service material. B is the highest ceiling that matches Oxford's editorial voice; D is the highest ceiling we can ship today without hiring anyone. Pick one of those two lanes and the Round 2 illustration budget becomes a single, scoped decision. (Reference C — Wardian-case / terrarium — was removed during founder review: Oxford does core aeration but doesn't collect display samples, so the motif implies a practice that doesn't exist.)

Round 1 ships
The existing code-drawn diagram on the Process page (unchanged). These references do not replace it.
Round 2 decision
Commission B or productize D. Not both. The illustration style carries the brand — having two is worse than having neither.
What this file is
A ceiling. The point is to look at these four and say "yes, that's the level of craft I'm paying for" (or "no, less than that"). Concept only.
What this file isn't
Production art. No illustrator has been briefed. No commission has been scoped. No cost has been committed. Every panel is stamped.
