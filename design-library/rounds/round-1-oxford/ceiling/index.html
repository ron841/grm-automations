The problem, stated plainly

You can mow a dying lawn until the mower breaks. It won't fix the soil.

The grass you see is the top half inch. Everything that makes grass grow — water movement, oxygen, nutrient exchange, root depth — happens in the four inches underneath. In Central Florida, that four inches is compacted sand, choked by thatch, starved of organic matter. Irrigation runs off. Fertilizer leaches past the root zone and into the aquifer. The symptoms look like a grass problem. They are a soil problem.

Oxford Lawn does one thing. We rebuild the soil underneath tired grass so the grass has something to live in. Three mechanical steps, one organic top-dressing, eighteen years of doing it across Sumter, Lake, and Marion County.

If your lawn is failing and nobody has offered you a reason other than "more fertilizer," you are the customer we were built for.

Feature · the rehab, step by step

One process. Three steps. Organic throughout.

Each step only works because of the step before it. Skip dethatching and the aeration plugs can't reach. Skip aeration and compost sits on top.

Step one · 0 – 1 in

Dethatching & short cutting

Thatch is where fungus and pests hide. Dense mat traps water on the surface and blocks everything we apply after. We strip it down so the next two steps can actually reach the soil.

Equipment · dethatcher ~1 day

Step two · 1 – 4 in

Core aeration

Machine-pulled plugs of sand and clay out of the root zone. Roots breathe. Water and compost reach the places they couldn't before. This is the part most lawn companies don't bother with.

About · voice: The Front Porch

Two people, a trailer, and a stubborn theory about soil.

Tom O'Brien started Oxford Lawn in 2008, which was not a great year to start anything. He kept it small on purpose. One truck, good equipment, the three-step rehab he had worked out by hand on his own properties first. Tracy handles the calls and the assessments. Between the two of them, they have been on more lawns in The Villages than most people have walked across.

The company has never chased volume. They turn down jobs where the soil problem is not the real problem. They don't sell ongoing maintenance contracts. If your lawn comes back after the rehab, you don't need them anymore. That is on purpose.

That is why we are a rehab specialist and not a lawn care company. And why, eighteen years in, we still only do the one thing.
