The story of a small company that stayed small on purpose.

Tom O'Brien started Oxford Lawn in 2008 out of a driveway in Wildwood. The recession was on. Nobody was hiring, and nobody wanted to spend money on their lawn, which was why it felt like the right time. If the business could work then, Tom figured, it could work anytime.

It worked. Not fast, and not loud. Tom kept the crew tight, the truck clean, and the service list short. He had worked out a three-step rehab process on his own properties before he ever showed it to a customer, and he was stubborn about not selling anything else. No weekly mowing. No chemical lawn program. Just the rehab, done right, on weak lawns that nobody else wanted to take on.

Tracy came on to handle the front of the business, which is most of the business. She is the one who answers the phone, drives the assessment visits, and talks people through what the rehab is and what it is not. If you have ever called Oxford Lawn, you have talked to Tracy.

The rehab theory, in Tom's words.

"People call because the grass looks bad. I tell them up front, the grass is not the problem. The grass is the symptom. By the time a St. Augustine lawn is thin and browning in patches in The Villages, the soil under it has been compacted for years. Irrigation water cannot move. Roots cannot reach. Fertilizer is going into the water table."

"So we pull the thatch. We aerate. We top-dress with compost. Every one of those is a physical action on the soil, not a chemical one. The grass does not know what hit it, in a good way. It starts growing because it finally can."

"If your lawn comes back after the rehab, you don't need us anymore. That is on purpose."
— Tom O'Brien, founder

Why no weekly mowing, no chemical program.

Oxford Lawn is a specialist, not a maintenance company. The rehab is the whole offering. When the rehab works, a lot of customers can go back to their regular mowing service and be fine for a long time. Some call us back in three years for a touch-up. Some never need to.

That is why 132 Google reviews in eighteen years is the number, not 1,320. Every review is from a lawn we actually rehabbed.
